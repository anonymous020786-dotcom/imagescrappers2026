Photos often carry hidden metadata: the camera and lens, the exact date and time, and sometimes the **GPS location** where they were taken. Image Scraper Pro shows you what's inside a photo and can remove it from everything you download.

## See what a photo reveals

1. Open an image in the dashboard's preview.
2. Click **Photo info**.

You'll see the camera, lens, exposure settings, date and, if the file contains them, the GPS coordinates. A warning appears when a photo reveals where it was taken. It works for JPEG, PNG and WebP files.

## Remove metadata from every download

Open the options (⚙) and turn on **Remove location and camera metadata (EXIF, GPS, XMP) from downloaded photos**. From then on, every **Download** and **Download ZIP**:

- strips EXIF (including GPS), XMP, comments and PNG text chunks from JPEG, PNG and WebP files
- cleans JPEG and PNG **losslessly**, with no re-compression, and keeps colour profiles so colours stay right
- re-encodes a rotated JPEG upright, so it doesn't appear sideways once the orientation tag is gone

Only your downloaded copy changes. The original on the website is untouched.

> **Tip:** Converting to another format, resizing or using **Prepare…** redraws the image, which also drops its metadata.

## When removing metadata matters

- Sharing photos taken at home, so you don't publish your address.
- Sending images to clients without camera serial numbers or editing history.
- Publishing images on your own website: smaller files and no personal data.

## Need credit details instead?

Metadata can also hold useful information: the creator, copyright and licence. Image Scraper Pro's licence and credit finder shows what a page or file states, after **Analyze**. Note that it reports what a source says, and it isn't legal advice.
